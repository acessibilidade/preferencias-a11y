Accessibility Statement

The website and tool Accessibility Preferences are an initiative by Tudo é Acessibilidade in partnership with EngTeca. The project demonstrates how to offer free presentation adjustments and explains why a tool like this does not replace the work of removing barriers.

This website has been manually tested and evaluated and complies with the main requirements related to the LBI (Brazilian Inclusion Law), Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act (United States), EN 301 549 (European Union), and other legislation in other parts of the world.

This statement covers this website and the preferences panel available here. Installing the panel on another site does not make that site accessible or transfer the practices described on this page to it.

Your experience helps identify barriers.

Have you encountered a problem here?

If any content or control makes it difficult or impossible to use the website or panel, write to oi@tudoeacessibilidade.com.br . You can also ask for help accessing the information.

To help us understand the barrier, please include the following, if possible:

  • The page and control you were using.
  • What you tried to do and what happened.
  • The browser, device, and assistive technology you used.
  • Which preferences were active when the problem occurred.

You do not need to know technical terms to contact us. If the problem occurred on another website using the tool, please include that site's address too.

How do we approach accessibility?

Care begins with the structure of the pages and interaction with each control. Reading adjustments are an additional part of the experience.

What have we done on the website and in this tool?

  • Structure and navigation: we organize content using headings, navigation regions, and main content, plus a link to skip directly to the content.
  • Labeled controls: we use native buttons, checkboxes, option lists, and expandable groups, with associated labels and visible focus indicators.
  • Opening and closing the panel: when it opens, focus moves to its heading. Closing it or pressing Escape returns focus to the trigger when it remains available. Moving focus outside closes the panel without pulling focus back.
  • Identifiable preferences: groups with nondefault settings display the text “Configured”, even when collapsed. The information does not rely on color alone.
  • Movement without relying on dragging: the floating panel provides direction buttons and arrow-key movement on the “Move panel” button. We include instructions, an approximate position description, and status messages about movement and screen boundaries.
  • Links and images: logos have alternative text. Decorative illustrations do not add repetitive descriptions. Links that open a new tab and shortcuts within the page include additional information for screen readers.
  • Adaptable presentation: we use relative text sizes, layouts that rearrange on small screens, and internal panel scrolling to reach its controls.
  • Movement during navigation: smooth scrolling for anchor links respects the system's reduced-motion preference. The panel also offers pausing for supported animations and videos, and muting for HTML audio and video.
  • Reversible choices: each preference can be changed individually or reset using “Restore defaults”. Content adjustments are saved in the browser, per site; the tool continues to work when storage is unavailable.

Monitoring and improvement

We check keyboard navigation, panel opening and closing, applying and restoring preferences, movement messages, and presentation on small screens.

Spoken movement messages were validated using the VoiceOver screen reader. Having a message in the code does not, by itself, guarantee the same experience across all browser and assistive technology combinations. Specific testing is important.

Reports from people using the site help guide our fixes. This statement describes the current implementation and its limitations.

Limitations to consider

The tool does not create image descriptions, fix forms, or reorganize page semantics. Font, color, spacing, and zoom adjustments depend on the site's HTML and CSS and may require manual review. Magnification can cause horizontal scrolling.

Muting and pausing do not control every format: embedded third-party players, Web Audio, GIFs, canvas, and some scripted movement may remain active. The tool's zoom also does not replace native browser zoom.

Observing, testing, and improving are part of the work.

Beyond preferences

Prioritize team training, participation by people with disabilities, and removing barriers within the project itself. To understand the limits of overlay tools' promises, visit Do not believe in miracle accessibility tools (opens in a new tab) .
